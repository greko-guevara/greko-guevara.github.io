## Teaching and Academic Education

I am a university professor with extensive experience teaching irrigation,
hydraulics, and applied programming within agricultural engineering programs.
My teaching philosophy integrates **engineering fundamentals, computational
thinking, and real-world agricultural systems**, with a strong emphasis on
learning by doing.

I aim to prepare students not only to understand concepts, but to **apply,
model, and communicate solutions** to complex water and soil-related problems.

---

## STEM Education and Digital Agriculture

A central component of my academic work is the integration of **STEM education
and digital tools** into agricultural engineering.

This includes:

- Teaching **programming for engineers** using Python & Excel
- Introducing data analysis and visualization for agricultural systems
- Promoting computational thinking as a core engineering skill
- Applying digital tools to irrigation, hydrology, and soil–water processes

I emphasize the use of **open-source tools** and reproducible workflows to
empower students to explore, test, and understand physical processes rather
than relying on black-box software.

---

## Teaching Methodology

My teaching approach is based on:

- **Conceptual clarity:** strong emphasis on physical meaning and assumptions
- **Applied learning:** field data, real datasets, and engineering case studies
- **Computational integration:** linking theory with numerical models
- **Critical thinking:** encouraging students to question results and methods

This methodology is particularly aligned with engineering education in
agricultural and environmental contexts.

## Educational Philosophy

I view education as a process of **building analytical capacity**, not merely
transmitting information. My goal is to train engineers and professionals who
can integrate theory, data, and computation to make informed decisions in
agricultural water management and soil–water systems.
